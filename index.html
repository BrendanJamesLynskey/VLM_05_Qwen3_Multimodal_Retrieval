01

Why Multimodal Retrieval — the Limits of Text-Only RAG

Classic RAG embeds text chunks with a text-only encoder (BGE, E5, Cohere v3, OpenAI text-embedding-3) and feeds the top-K back to a text LLM. That pipeline silently collapses every time the source corpus is fundamentally visual: scanned PDFs, screenshots, tables-as-images, slide decks, datasheets, schematics, photographs, product imagery, dashboards.

Two common workarounds, both lossy:

  • OCR-then-embed. Run Tesseract / docTR / Azure Document Intelligence, embed the resulting text. Loses layout, charts, diagrams, handwriting, low-resolution scans — and OCR errors propagate into retrieval recall.
  • Caption-then-embed. Have a VLM describe each image in text, embed the caption. Adds compute, hallucinates, and discards any detail the captioner didn’t happen to mention.

The principled fix is to embed pixels and text into a shared vector space, retrieve directly against the original modality, and let a VLM read the original evidence to answer. The Qwen3 family is unusual in that it ships three matched components designed to compose into exactly this pipeline.

Why one family

Using a single family for embedding, reranking, and generation buys two things: (i) shared instruction-following conventions (every component understands <|im_start|> / <|im_end|> and the same instruction prefix style), and (ii) operational simplicity — one tokeniser, one weight format (Apache-2.0 open weights), one inference runtime (vLLM, SGLang, Transformers all support the entire family).

The Qwen3 Family at a Glance — Three Models, One Recipe

All three components are decoder-only transformers initialised from the same Qwen3 backbone, then post-trained with different objectives. They share tokeniser, RoPE configuration, and instruction template — differences are in the head and the loss.

Qwen3-EmbeddingBi-encoder retrieval (text−text)0.6B / 4B / 8BDense vector (up to 4096-d, MRL-truncatable to 32…)Last-hidden EOS pooling + InfoNCE contrastive

A single instruction template

All four components accept an optional task instruction via the same Instruct: prefix convention. For the embedder it shapes the query side of the contrastive geometry; for the reranker it shapes the yes/no judgement; for Qwen3-VL it is just the system prompt. Same string format, three different downstream effects — this is why the family composes cleanly.

03

End-to-End Pipeline — Embed, Search, Rerank, Generate

The retrieval pipeline is two-stage by design: a cheap recall stage (bi-encoder + ANN) over the whole corpus, then a precise rescoring stage (cross-encoder) on a small candidate set, then generation. The split exists because bi-encoders are fast but lossy and cross-encoders are accurate but quadratic.

User query (text and/or image)
↓
Qwen3-Embedding-VL  (or text-only)
↓
Query vector  q ∈ ℝd
↓
ANN search over corpus vectors  (HNSW / IVF-PQ)
↓
Top-N candidates  (N ≈ 50–200)
↓
Qwen3-Reranker  (cross-encoder, N forward passes)
↓
Top-K reranked  (K ≈ 3–10)
↓
Qwen3-VL  (generation, grounded on top-K)
↓
Final answer with citations

Two properties make this work:

  • Decoupled cost. The bi-encoder runs once per document at ingest and once per query at runtime — the corpus encoding is amortised. The cross-encoder runs N times per query but only over the top-N, not the whole corpus.
  • Decoupled accuracy. The bi-encoder optimises for recall@N (does the right document appear at all?). The cross-encoder optimises for precision@K (is the ordering inside the top-N correct?). They train on different losses on purpose.
04

Qwen3-VL — the Multimodal Generator

Qwen3-VL is the generative VLM at the end of the pipeline. It consumes the reranked top-K (text passages and/or full images) plus the user query and produces a grounded answer. Architecturally it inherits the Qwen2.5-VL design and pushes it further:

Visual encoder

A ViT trained with native-resolution support (no fixed tile grid). Patch tokens carry M-RoPE — multimodal rotary embeddings with separate frequency bands for time, height, and width — so the LLM can reason about positions in 2D images and 3D video volumes without retraining for each modality.

Token budget

Image tokens are produced at native resolution and can range from 256 to ~16k per image. A min_pixels / max_pixels control lets you cap the budget per call — essential when you are stuffing top-K full-page PDFs into one prompt.

Sizes

Dense variants (2B, 4B, 7B, 32B, 72B) for self-hosting, and large MoE variants (Qwen3-VL-A3B activating 3B/30B total, Qwen3-VL-A22B activating 22B/235B total) for frontier-quality cloud use. Same tokeniser and chat template across all sizes.

Long context

Up to 256k native, extensible to ~1M with YaRN. Matters here because retrieved evidence is bulky: ten reranked PDF pages at 2k visual tokens each is already 20k tokens before the question. Long context is what makes ‘rerank to K=10’ viable instead of K=3.

Why Qwen3-VL specifically as the generator

Two practical reasons. First, its document understanding ability (OCR, layout, charts, tables) is strong enough that you can hand it raw page images without pre-OCR. Second, its bounding-box grounding output format (the model can return [x1,y1,x2,y2] citations) lets the application highlight the source region in the original document, which is exactly what users want from a multimodal RAG.

05

Qwen3-Embedding (and the VL Variant) — Bi-Encoder Geometry

Qwen3-Embedding is a Qwen3 decoder repurposed as an encoder: it processes one input at a time and emits a single fixed-length vector. The text-only models (0.6B / 4B / 8B) lead MTEB at their release; the VL variant applies the same recipe to a Qwen3-VL backbone, embedding images and text into a shared space.

How a decoder becomes an embedder

  • Pooling. Take the last-layer hidden state at the final <|endoftext|> position. (Last-token pooling beats mean-pooling for decoder-only models because causal attention concentrates information at the end.)
  • Normalise. L2-normalise to unit length so cosine similarity = dot product.
  • Contrastive training. InfoNCE loss with in-batch negatives plus hard negatives mined from a first-pass retriever. Three-stage curriculum: weakly-supervised pretraining on synthetic pairs → supervised SFT on labelled retrieval data → model merging across checkpoints.
  • Instruction-aware. An optional Instruct: {task}\nQuery: {q} prefix conditions the embedding on the task — the same model can produce different vectors for ‘classify sentiment’ vs ‘retrieve similar product’.

The VL extension

For Qwen3-Embedding-VL, the same recipe runs on a Qwen3-VL backbone. An input can be text, an image, or interleaved image+text. Patch tokens enter the sequence with M-RoPE; the final pooled vector lives in the same d-dimensional space as a text-only query vector. This is what makes text→image and image→text retrieval work in one index without modality-specific projection heads.

Matryoshka Representation Learning (MRL)

Vectors are trained so that prefixes are also valid embeddings. The 8B model emits a 4096-d vector; you can truncate to 1024, 512, 256, even 128 and lose accuracy gracefully instead of catastrophically. In production this lets you store small vectors in the ANN index (e.g. 256-d for HNSW recall) and full 4096-d only when needed.

06

ANN Search — from Vectors to Top-N Candidates

The embedder is one half of the recall stage; the other is an approximate-nearest-neighbour index. Qwen3-Embedding-VL outputs do not care which index you use — they are just float vectors — but the choice has real consequences at scale.

HNSW

Hierarchical Navigable Small World graphs. Best recall/latency at <10M vectors. In-memory; RAM grows linearly with corpus. Default in qdrant, weaviate, pgvector.

IVF-PQ

Inverted file with product quantisation. Compresses vectors 8–32× with controlled accuracy loss. The right answer for 100M+ vectors. FAISS, Milvus.

DiskANN

Graph index that lives on SSD with a small RAM cache. Billion-scale on a single node. The right answer when corpus > RAM budget.

Two interactions specific to Qwen3-Embedding-VL matter:

  • Pick the MRL dimension to match the index. Storing 4096-d HNSW at 100M docs is ~1.6 TB of vectors alone. Truncating to 512-d and re-normalising gives 200 GB for typically <1% recall loss — almost always the right move.
  • One index, both modalities. Because images and text share the space, you do not need separate indexes for ‘the image collection’ and ‘the text collection’. A text query finds relevant images and vice-versa from one ANN call.
What the recall stage gets wrong

Bi-encoders compress each side into one vector, so they cannot model fine-grained interaction between query and document. A query mentioning ‘Q3 EU revenue 2024’ will pull back pages mentioning any of those tokens; whether the page actually answers the question is something a single dot product cannot tell. That is what the next stage is for.

07

Qwen3-Reranker — Cross-Encoder Precision

The reranker is architecturally identical to Qwen3-Embedding but trained with a different head and loss. Instead of producing a vector, it consumes the (query, candidate) pair jointly in the same forward pass and emits a single relevance score.

Bi-encoder (the embedder)

score(q, d) = cos(f(q), f(d))

Each side encoded independently. Cheap: O(|corpus|) at ingest, O(1) at query. Document vectors precomputed and reused.

  • Cannot model token-level query-document interaction
  • One vector compresses away discriminating detail
  • Strong on recall, weak on precision

Cross-encoder (the reranker)

score(q, d) = head(f([q; d]))

Concatenated and passed through one transformer. Expensive: O(N) forward passes per query, no precomputation possible.

  • Full attention between query and document tokens
  • Captures negation, hedging, scope, numeric matches
  • Strong on precision — reorders the top-N decisively

The Qwen3-Reranker prompt format

The reranker is trained as a pointwise yes/no classifier wrapped in a chat prompt. At inference, the model sees a prompt like ‘Given a query and a document, judge whether the document meets the requirement. Answer only yes or no.’ The score is the logit (or probability) it assigns to the yes token.

qwen3_reranker.py — how the score is actually computed
# Build the chat-template prompt the reranker was trained on
prompt = format_instruction(
    instruction="Given a web search query, retrieve relevant passages.",
    query=q, doc=d,
)

# Run one forward pass; we only need the logits at the last position
logits = model(prompt).logits[0, -1]

# Pull out the two relevant token IDs and softmax over just those two
yes_id, no_id = tokenizer("yes").input_ids[0], tokenizer("no").input_ids[0]
score = torch.softmax(torch.stack([logits[no_id], logits[yes_id]]), dim=-1)[1]
Multimodal reranking

The same recipe runs on a Qwen3-VL backbone — the ‘document’ can be an image. This is the equivalent of ColPali at the rerank stage: cheap dense retrieval first, then a precise vision-aware cross-encoder over the top-N. In practice the gain over text-only rerank is largest when the corpus has visual evidence the embedder collapsed (charts, dense tables, scanned forms).

08

End-to-End Code — Putting It Together

The whole pipeline in ~40 lines. Embed at ingest, rerank at query time, generate over the reranked top-K. The example uses Hugging Face Transformers; in production swap to vLLM for the embedder/reranker and SGLang or vLLM for Qwen3-VL.

multimodal_rag.py
from qwen_embed import Qwen3EmbeddingVL, Qwen3Reranker
from transformers import AutoProcessor, Qwen3VLForConditionalGeneration
import faiss, torch

# ---- 1. Ingest: embed every page (image or text) once ----
embedder = Qwen3EmbeddingVL.from_pretrained("Qwen/Qwen3-Embedding-VL-7B")
corpus_vecs = embedder.encode(corpus_items, dim=512)        # MRL truncation

index = faiss.IndexHNSWFlat(512, 32)
index.add(corpus_vecs)

# ---- 2. Query: text or image, same call ----
q_vec   = embedder.encode([query], dim=512,
                          instruction="Find pages that answer this question.")
_, idxs = index.search(q_vec, k=100)                     # Top-N recall
candidates = [corpus_items[i] for i in idxs[0]]

# ---- 3. Rerank: cross-encoder over 100 candidates ----
reranker = Qwen3Reranker.from_pretrained("Qwen/Qwen3-Reranker-4B")
scores   = reranker.score(query, candidates,
                          instruction="Judge whether the page answers the query.")
top_k    = [candidates[i] for i in torch.topk(scores, k=5).indices]

# ---- 4. Generate: ground Qwen3-VL on the reranked evidence ----
vlm  = Qwen3VLForConditionalGeneration.from_pretrained("Qwen/Qwen3-VL-7B-Instruct")
proc = AutoProcessor.from_pretrained("Qwen/Qwen3-VL-7B-Instruct")

messages = [{"role": "user", "content": [
    *[{"type": "image", "image": c.image} for c in top_k if c.is_image],
    *[{"type": "text",  "text":  c.text}   for c in top_k if not c.is_image],
    {"type": "text", "text": f"Answer using only the evidence above: {query}"},
]}]

inputs = proc.apply_chat_template(messages, return_tensors="pt", tokenize=True,
                                  add_generation_prompt=True)
out    = vlm.generate(**inputs, max_new_tokens=512)
answer = proc.decode(out[0], skip_special_tokens=True)
Two production refinements that matter

Hybrid recall. Run BM25 in parallel with the dense embedder and fuse with Reciprocal Rank Fusion before rerank. BM25 catches exact-string queries the embedder smooths over.

Score thresholding. The reranker score is calibrated (it’s a yes-probability). Drop candidates below 0.2 rather than forcing a fixed K — some queries genuinely have only one relevant page.

Sizing, Latency & Cost — Picking the Right Variants

Each stage has independent sizing. The embedder runs millions of times at ingest, the reranker dozens of times per query, the VLM once. Their cost-per-call dictates which size you can afford at each stage.

StageCalls per queryLatency targetRecommended Qwen3 variantWhy this size
Ingest embed0 (offline)n/a (batch)Embedding-VL-7B at full dim, then MRL-truncateOne-time cost; pay for best vectors
Query embed1<50 msEmbedding-VL-2B (or 7B INT8)On the critical path of every request
ANN search1<20 msn/a (HNSW/IVF-PQ)Bound by index, not by Qwen3
RerankN (50–200)<500 ms totalReranker-0.6B or 4B0.6B handles 100 pairs in ~200 ms on one A100; 4B for hard corpora
Generate1<3 s first tokenQwen3-VL-7B (latency) or Qwen3-VL-A3B MoE (quality)The expensive call — only one of them

Three asymmetries to internalise

  • Embedder size doesn’t scale linearly with quality. Going 2B→7B at the embedder typically buys 1–2 MTEB points; going 0.6B→4B at the reranker often buys 5–10 nDCG points. Spend the parameter budget on the reranker.
  • MRL dim matters more than parameter count. A 7B embedder truncated to 256-d outperforms a 2B embedder at native 1024-d on most corpora, while costing the same to index. Always evaluate at the dim you will actually deploy.
  • Rerank top-N is the real knob. Doubling N from 50 to 100 typically improves end-to-end recall@K more than upgrading any individual component. Reranker compute is cheap relative to the VLM that follows.
When to skip the reranker

If the bi-encoder’s top-K and top-5N agree on the same documents (measure this: it’s the ‘rank stability’ metric), the reranker is buying nothing. This happens on small, lexically clean corpora — product catalogues, FAQ knowledge bases. Save the 500 ms.

10

What to Take Away

  • Multimodal retrieval needs a shared embedding space, not OCR or captioning. Qwen3-Embedding-VL gives you that space directly — one index, text queries find images, image queries find text.
  • Embedders and rerankers are two halves of the same recall–precision trade-off. Bi-encoders are O(1) per query but lossy; cross-encoders see both sides jointly but cost O(N). Use both, in that order, every time.
  • The Qwen3 family composes because all three components share one backbone, one tokeniser, and one instruction format. The instruction prefix conditions the embedder, the reranker, and the VLM in different ways from the same string.
  • Matryoshka representations decouple model size from storage cost. Train at 4096-d, serve at 256-d, lose <1% recall. This is the single biggest lever for retrieval economics at >10M documents.
  • Spend parameters on the reranker, not the embedder. 0.6B→4B at the reranker buys more end-to-end quality than 2B→7B at the embedder.
  • The reranker score is calibrated — treat it as a probability. Threshold rather than fixing K; you save VLM tokens on easy queries and admit ‘no relevant document’ on hard ones.
  • Qwen3-VL closes the loop. Long context (256k+) lets you actually feed the top-K reranked pages as full images, and bounding-box outputs let the UI cite back into the source document. That is what makes the whole pipeline feel grounded rather than just plausible.
